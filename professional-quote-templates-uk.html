2. Microsoft Word template - best for occasional quotes on a laptop

A Word quote template is the classic starting point. Drop in your logo, type the work, save as a PDF.

Pros:

  • Most people already have Word
  • Full control over wording
  • Fine for the occasional quote

Cons:

  • Slow to fill in by hand every time
  • Layout drifts the moment you add or remove lines
  • No automatic line totals or VAT maths
  • Awkward to do on a phone on site

Best for: trades who only quote now and then and are happy working at a laptop.

3. Microsoft Excel template - best for line-item maths

If your quotes involve a lot of measured items, an Excel template lets the spreadsheet add everything up for you.

Pros:

  • Automatic subtotals and totals once set up
  • Good for material-heavy jobs

Cons:

  • Spreadsheets rarely look professional when printed to PDF
  • Easy to break a formula and not notice
  • Genuinely painful on a phone
  • Needs setting up before it is any use

Best for: trades comfortable with spreadsheets who quote lots of itemised work and do not mind plainer-looking output.

4. Google Docs template - best for quoting across devices

A Google Docs or Sheets template gives you the Word or Excel experience with the bonus of working across your phone, tablet and laptop through the cloud.

Pros:

  • Cloud document tools you can access anywhere
  • Edit on any device

Cons:

  • Same formatting headaches as Word and Excel
  • Still manual, still slow
  • You do the layout and the maths yourself

Best for: trades who want to start a quote on the laptop and finish it on their phone without paying for software.

5. Canva quote template - best for design-conscious trades

Canva offers attractive quote templates if presentation matters to you and you have a little time to spend.

Pros:

  • Genuinely smart-looking templates
  • Easy to add branding and colour

Cons:

  • Built for designers, not for fast quoting
  • No quoting logic - you type and total everything yourself
  • The good features sit behind a paid plan
  • Slow for someone quoting several jobs a week

Best for: trades who care a lot about how the document looks and quote a low volume of high-value jobs.

6. Accounting software quotes - best if you already run full accounts software

Full accounts packages can produce quotes that convert into invoices. If you already pay for one, the quoting is bundled in.

Pros:

  • Quote flows straight into invoicing
  • Professional output
  • One system for quotes and accounts

Cons:

  • Overkill if you only want to quote
  • The quote builder is often a minor afterthought feature
  • Mid to high monthly cost
  • More to learn than a focused quoting tool

Best for: established firms already committed to a full accounts platform.

7. Trade CRM quotes - best for larger firms wanting everything in one place

All-in-one trade CRM platforms bundle quoting with job management, scheduling and invoicing.

Pros:

  • Quotes, jobs and invoices in one system
  • Good for teams

Cons:

  • Heavy to set up and learn
  • You pay for a lot of features beyond quoting
  • Higher monthly cost
  • Slower to get your first quote out the door

Best for: larger outfits with staff, multiple jobs running, and a need for full job management.
