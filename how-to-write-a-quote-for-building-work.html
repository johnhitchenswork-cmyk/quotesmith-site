How to Write a Quote for Building Work: Step-by-Step Guide (2026)

A good quote does two jobs. It tells the customer exactly what they are paying for, and it tells them you are a proper tradesperson who runs a proper business. Get it right and you win more of the work you price up. Get it wrong, or scribble it on the back of a job sheet, and the customer quietly goes with whoever looked more organised.

This guide walks you through writing a clear, professional quote for building work in seven steps, whether you are a builder, plumber, electrician, plasterer or painter. By the end you will know exactly what to put in, how to lay it out, and how to send it so it actually wins the job.

Estimated read: about 8 minutes. No experience needed.

Quote or estimate? Get this straight first

These two words are not the same, and mixing them up causes arguments later.

  • A quote is a fixed price. You are committing to do the described work for that figure. Use a quote when you can see the full scope and you are confident on materials and time.
  • An estimate is your best informed guess. The final figure can move if the job changes. Use an estimate when there are unknowns, like what is behind a wall or under a floor.

If you are not sure, say so on the document and label it clearly. Customers respect honesty far more than a surprise bill.

What you need before you start

  • The full scope of the job, measured and noted on site
  • Your material prices (and a quick call to your merchant if prices have moved)
  • Your day rate or labour pricing
  • Your business details: name, address, phone, email, and VAT number if you are VAT registered
  • Your logo if you have one
  • Estimated time: about 15 minutes by hand, or about two minutes with quoting software

Step 1: Put your business details at the top

Start with who you are. A quote that opens with a clear, branded header instantly looks more established than a figure texted over.

Include:

  1. Your trading name and logo
  2. Address, phone number and email
  3. Your VAT number if you are VAT registered
  4. A quote number and the date

You should see: a clean header that looks like it came from a real firm, not a one-man scribble.

Note: a quote number matters more than people think. It makes you look organised and gives you both a reference when the customer calls back three weeks later.

Step 2: Add the customer's details and the job address

Underneath your header, add the customer's name, the site address and the date you visited. This anchors the quote to one specific job so there is no confusion later if you are quoting several at once.

Step 3: Describe the work in plain, itemised lines

This is the part most trades rush, and it is the part that wins or loses the job. Do not write "supply and fit bathroom - GBP 4,200" as a single line. Break it down.

List each element of the work on its own line:

  1. The task in plain English
  2. What is included (materials, labour, removal of waste)
  3. The price for that line

For example, a bathroom job might list: strip out and dispose of old suite, first fix plumbing, supply and fit new suite, tile walls and floor, second fix and test, make good and clean.

You should see: a customer who can read exactly what they are getting for their money. Itemised quotes feel fair. A single big number feels like a gamble, and people do not gamble with their homes.

Step 4: Be clear on what is NOT included

Protect yourself and the customer by spelling out exclusions. This is where late arguments come from, so head them off now.

Common exclusions to state:

  • Making good to decoration unless listed
  • Unforeseen work behind walls or under floors
  • Removal of materials containing asbestos
  • Parking or permit costs on restricted streets

A short "What this quote does not cover" section saves you a painful conversation down the line.

Step 5: Handle materials, labour and VAT correctly

Decide how you are showing the numbers and be consistent:

  1. Show materials and labour either combined per line or split out, but pick one approach
  2. Add a clear subtotal
  3. If you are VAT registered, show the VAT rate and amount as a separate line, then the total including VAT
  4. If you are not VAT registered, say so or simply show the total with no VAT line
Note: never bury VAT. A customer who thinks the total is the price and then gets a bill 20 percent higher will not pay it happily, and they will tell their neighbours.

Step 6: Add your terms, timing and payment details

A professional quote sets expectations before the work starts. Add a short terms section covering:

  • How long the quote is valid for (28 days is common, as material prices move)
  • Rough start date or lead time
  • Payment terms: any deposit, stage payments, and when the balance is due
  • Accepted payment methods

Clear terms make you look established and make getting paid far easier.

Step 7: Make it look the part, then send it fast

Two quotes can contain the same numbers and the same words, and the tidy, branded one wins. Presentation is not vanity. It is the customer's only signal of how you will treat their home.

  1. Lay it out cleanly with your branding
  2. Save it as a PDF so it cannot be edited and looks the same on any device
  3. Send it the same day if you can, while the customer is still keen

You should see: a document you would be happy to receive yourself. Then send it before the other two quotes land. Speed wins jobs as often as price does.

Frequently asked questions

What should a quote for building work include?

Your business details, a quote number and date, the customer and site details, an itemised description of the work, what is not included, materials, labour and VAT, a total, and your terms covering validity, timing and payment.

How long should a building quote be valid for?

28 days is common, because material prices move. State the validity clearly so there is no argument if the customer comes back two months later.

What is the difference between a quote and an estimate?

A quote is a fixed price you commit to. An estimate is your best informed guess that can change if the job changes. Always label which one you are giving.

Do I need to show VAT on my quote?

If you are VAT registered, yes, show the VAT rate and amount as a separate line, then the total including VAT. If you are not registered, you do not charge VAT.
