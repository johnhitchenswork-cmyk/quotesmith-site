How to Get Paid Faster as a Tradesperson: 9 Ways to Fix Cash Flow (2026)

You can be the best tradesperson on the street and still struggle, simply because the money comes in too slowly. The work is done, the customer is delighted, and yet you are stood at the merchant's counter wondering whether to put the next job's materials on the card. Late payment is the quiet killer of small trade businesses.

The good news is that getting paid faster is mostly about habits, not luck. This guide walks through nine practical changes you can make, from taking a deposit before you start to chasing an overdue invoice without sounding desperate. None of it needs a lawyer or a hard conversation.

Estimated read: about 8 minutes. Built for UK sole traders and small firms.

Why trades get paid late in the first place

Before fixing it, it helps to know why it happens. Late payment rarely means the customer is dishonest. Usually it is one of these:

  • There was no clear payment plan agreed before the work started.
  • The invoice was vague, late, or hard to pay.
  • The customer simply forgot and there was no polite nudge.
  • A dispute over scope was never headed off in the quote.

Fix the system and most of these vanish. Here is how.

1. Agree the payment plan in the quote, not on the doorstep

The single biggest fix is to set out exactly how and when you will be paid before a tool comes out of the van. Put it in writing on the quote so there are no surprises. A clear payment schedule turns an awkward chat into something the customer already agreed to.

State the deposit, any stage payments, and when the final balance is due. If you need help laying this out cleanly, see our guide on how to write a quote for building work.

2. Take a deposit before starting work

Asking for a deposit is normal and expected for trade work in the UK. It covers your materials, proves the customer is serious, and means you are never fully out of pocket. A deposit of 25 to 50 percent on materials-heavy jobs is reasonable.

Note: frame the deposit as standard practice, because it is. "I take a deposit to cover materials, then stage the rest as the work progresses" sounds like a professional, not a worry.

3. Use stage payments on bigger jobs

For anything running more than a few days, break the payment into stages tied to milestones. You are never carrying weeks of unpaid labour, and the customer pays as they see progress.

DepositOn acceptance, before start30 percent
Mid-pointFirst fix complete40 percent
FinalOn completion and sign-off30 percent

Agree these triggers in writing up front so each request is expected, not a surprise.

4. Invoice the same day the work finishes

Cash flow problems often start with the tradesperson, not the customer. If the invoice goes out two weeks late, payment follows two weeks late. Send it the day you finish, while the customer is still pleased with the result and the work is fresh in their mind.

5. Make the invoice easy to read and easy to pay

A clear invoice gets paid faster than a vague one. Include:

  1. Your business name, contact details and a unique invoice number.
  2. An itemised breakdown that matches the quote the customer agreed to.
  3. The total clearly stated, with VAT shown separately if you are registered.
  4. Your bank details right there on the invoice.
  5. A clear due date, not just "on receipt".

The fewer steps between the customer reading the invoice and paying it, the sooner the money lands.

6. Set clear, short payment terms

Decide your terms and put them on every invoice. Seven or fourteen days is common for domestic work. "Payment due within 7 days of completion" gives the customer a deadline and gives you a clear point at which to follow up.

7. Chase overdue invoices politely but firmly

Most late payments are forgetfulness, not refusal, so a calm reminder usually does the job. Stick to a simple sequence:

  • Day after due date: a friendly nudge. "Just a quick reminder that invoice 1042 was due yesterday. Let me know if you need the details again."
  • One week over: a firmer but polite follow-up restating the amount and due date.
  • Two weeks over: a clear message that the balance is now overdue and asking for a payment date.

Keep every message professional and unemotional. You are running a business and chasing what you are owed is a normal part of it.

8. Offer simple, fast ways to pay

If paying you means digging out a chequebook, expect delays. Make it effortless. Bank transfer with the details on the invoice is fine for most jobs. Whatever you accept, state it plainly so the customer never has to ask how to pay you.

9. Stop quotes going cold before they become jobs

Getting paid faster starts before the job, at the quote stage. A customer who never replies to your quote is a job that never pays at all. Quotes go cold for predictable reasons:

  • Too slow. By the time your quote lands, two tidier ones already have. Send it the same day.
  • Too vague. A single big number feels like a gamble. Itemised quotes feel fair and get a yes.
  • Too scruffy. A figure texted over loses to a clean, branded PDF every time.

Win the quote cleanly and the whole payment journey starts on the front foot.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal to ask for a deposit before starting work?

Yes. Deposits are standard for UK trade work, especially on materials-heavy jobs. They cover your outlay and confirm the customer is committed. A deposit of 25 to 50 percent is common.

How do I chase an unpaid invoice without sounding rude?

Start with a friendly reminder the day after the due date, then a firmer but polite follow-up a week later, then a clear overdue notice after two weeks. Keep every message calm and factual. Most late payment is simply forgetfulness.

What payment terms should a tradesperson use?

Seven or fourteen days from completion is common for domestic work. Put a clear due date on every invoice rather than just writing "on receipt".

Why do customers stop replying to my quotes?

Usually because the quote arrived too slowly, was too vague, or looked unprofessional next to a tidier one. Send an itemised, branded quote the same day to keep the job warm.

Should I use stage payments?

On any job running more than a few days, yes. Tie each payment to a milestone such as first fix or completion, and agree the stages in writing before you start.
